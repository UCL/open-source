As [WeeklyOSM 830][wOSM830] reminded us last week:

> [Muki Haklay][mh], Professor of Geographical Information Science in
> the UCL Department of Geography, and UCL alumnus [Patrick
> Weber][pw], have [won the IEEE’s Pervasive Computing’s Test of Time
> Award][UCLpr] for their [2008 paper on OpenStreetMap][haklay-weber-2008].
> The award honours the most influential papers that have had a
> lasting impact on pervasive computing over the past 25 years. Over
> the eighteen years since Muki’s paper was published the
> OpenStreetMap project, which was launched at UCL in 2004, has
> evolved into critical digital infrastructure that supports research,
> humanitarian response, navigation systems, smart cities, and
> countless location-based services.

This is quite a nice coincidence with the event that we will be
running next week as part of this year's [London Data Week][ldw].
Our [Mapping festival event][mapfest] will have three parallel
sessions to get your mapping curiosity satisfied.

It will include: mapping activities where you can help humanitarian
aid organizations to map areas before they go there to help;
workshops to learn how to use OpenStreetMap data to obtain insights
about a particular location;
and an exploratory activity around campus to improve the quality of
the map information about UCL.

If you are interested on any (or all!) of those activities,
[join us][mapfest] on July 7th, from 14:00 at the main campus.
